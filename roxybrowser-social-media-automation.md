# How to Automate Social Media Engagement with AI (Step 7)

Running more than a couple of social accounts means logging in, checking feeds, replying, and posting — over and over, per account. RoxyBrowser's AI Agent can take over the repetitive parts of that loop while each profile stays a separate, isolated device. Here's how to set it up responsibly.

## What AI automation should (and shouldn't) do

Automation is for the *repetitive, legitimate* work of managing accounts you own or are authorized to run:

| Good use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Scheduling and posting your own content | Mass fake engagement or buying interaction |
| Monitoring feeds and collecting mentions | Spamming comments across many accounts |
| Drafting replies for you to review | Auto-posting identical text from dozens of profiles |
| Collecting public research per account | Scraping at volumes that break platform rules |

The line that matters: automation should save *your* time on real work — not manufacture fake activity. Platforms detect and ban the latter, tool or no tool.

## Why antidetect isolation matters for automation

Every social platform links accounts by device signals. If your automation tool drives ten accounts from one normal browser, they all share one fingerprint — and the platform sees ten puppets on one string. RoxyBrowser gives each account its own cookies, proxy, and fingerprint, so each automated session looks like an independent device.

## Setting it up

1. **One profile per account.** Create a dedicated RoxyBrowser profile for each social account, named clearly (for example `acme-ig-main`).
2. **Attach a residential proxy per profile**, with timezone and language matched to the proxy's exit location.
3. **Log into each account once** inside its own profile so the session persists.
4. **Open the AI Agent** from the profile menu and pick a task — for example, monitoring a feed for mentions or drafting post replies.
5. **Start with read-and-draft tasks.** Let the AI collect and draft, and keep human review on anything that gets published.
6. **Keep a human in the loop** on anything customer-facing, at least until you trust the output.

## A realistic automation workflow

1. **Morning scan:** the AI Agent checks each account's feed and collects mentions, comments, and trending topics into a summary.
2. **You review the summary** and approve the items worth acting on.
3. **Drafting:** the AI drafts replies or post ideas per account, in that account's voice.
4. **You edit and approve** — one pass, not per-account drudgery.
5. **Publishing:** approved content goes out from each account's own isolated profile.

The AI does the reading and the typing; you keep the judgment. That split is what keeps automation sustainable instead of ban-worthy.

## Common automation mistakes

- Running all accounts through one profile or one proxy — instant account-linking.
- Auto-publishing AI output without review — one bad reply, everywhere at once.
- Automating engagement itself (fake likes, mass comments) — the fastest way to lose accounts.
- Ignoring each platform's automation and scraping rules — they differ, and they change.
